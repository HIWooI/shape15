What comes out

A reference, not an action

The downstream whole-body policy observes 23 reference joint positions, 23 reference velocities, and a 6-D torso orientation, at 50 Hz.

No root position is needed, so calibration only matters for the display. Velocity is ours to produce — and differencing divides target jitter by dt, which is why a single IK branch flip once became 55 rad/s.

Feasibility stays with the policy. Contact, self-collision, and balance are deliberately off the critical path.

Pipeline · measured per stage on one RTX 5090

Finding 01

The student lacked information, not capacity

Trained on 14 keypoints, the network saturates at 15.4° — and width, depth, more data, and temporal context all fail to move it.

Given body_pose, the 76-joint skeleton rotation the teacher itself consumes and which the perception stack already emits for free, it drops to 9.5° in one step.

Finding 02 · the central result

Cross-limb contamination is structural, so the fix is structural

Raising only the arms moved the legs. A monolithic network maps 228 input dimensions to all 23 joints at once, so every input touches every output. Adding mirror augmentation made both legs react equally — symmetry improved, total leakage did not (9.66° → 9.89°). That was the wrong metric.

Cutting the input instead makes leakage zero by construction. Perturbing each input group by ±20° and measuring the leg output locates the path:

Cutting the clavicles left the larger path untouched. The contaminating channel was the spine — and perception is what opens it: in real output, spine and arm motion correlate at +0.737, and in the fastest 5 % of arm frames the spine moves 4.3× its usual amount. Raising an arm makes perception bend the spine, and the leg expert follows. Cost of closing it: 0.11°.

Synthetic data

The sampling scheme is the whole result

The teacher is a deterministic function of pose parameters, so labels can be made without video. Widening each joint range and sampling uniformly was worthless: off-manifold poses push the teacher's legs into their limits, and 41 % of the labels come back pathological.

Two counterintuitive effects: more synthetic data is worse — 1:1 with real data beats 3:1 — and the blend coefficient has an interior optimum, because 0.35 adds no new information and 1.00 is the midpoint of two poses and drifts toward the mean.

Calibration · and what still hurts

The residual error is not spread evenly. It concentrates in the twist degrees of freedom that position targets cannot determine — shoulder_yaw and wrist_roll worst — and in poses that are intrinsically hard rather than badly perceived.

Keypoint confidence is not the cause: the worst 5 % of frames average 0.86 against a global mean of 0.87. The solver sees 14 points — the joint orientations perception already produces are still being discarded, and that is the largest identified lever left.

Two failures worth reporting

A self-consistent teacher can still be wrong

Label quality was checked numerically only. The network memorised labels to 0.18° and nearest-neighbour label differences were 1.8°, from which we concluded the teacher was fine. Watching a human and the teacher side by side showed the left leg not matching the person within seconds.

The retargeter submodule was pinned to a revision whose own provenance field described it as an experimental candidate using another robot's stabiliser. Upgrading took the pathological-frame rate from 50.4 % to 1.0 % on the worst clip, and 26 % to 6.6 % across the dataset. Every contaminated label, model, and offset was deleted and regenerated.

Render before concluding. Statistics cannot see a consistent bias.

Standard deviation cannot tell left from right

A left-hip roll standard deviation 1.79× the right looked like teacher asymmetry. A mirror test cleared it: the disagreement between reflecting the input and reflecting the output is 0.62° mean, 0.05° median.

Per clip the asymmetry disappears — the global ratio is produced by two clips, taekwondo and cheerleading, whose deviations are 5–10× the rest and which are genuinely one-legged choreography. The earlier evidence that the human input was symmetric was invalid for the same reason: a standard deviation returns the same value whether the left foot or the right foot is the one being raised.
